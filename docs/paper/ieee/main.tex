\title{Spatial Gesture Control: Controlling a Laptop with Head Orientation and Smartwatch Pinch Gestures}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Vaibhav Bisht}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We present a system that lets a person control a laptop without touching it: they look at a calibrated screen location, pinch to grab a virtual volume knob, turn the wrist to change the volume and pinch again to release. Head orientation comes from consumer Sony headphones. Wrist motion and raw photoplethysmography (PPG) come from a Samsung Galaxy Watch over Bluetooth Low Energy, and all inference runs on the laptop behind a fail-closed safety chain. Because the watch cannot see the fingers, we recorded wrist signals with camera hand landmarks as timing ground truth and evaluated detectors as continuous event detectors, counting caught pinches against false activations per hour of no-gesture time. Wrist PPG alone generalised poorly to a new day (AUC 0.60--0.64). Fusing the inertial sensors raised worst-split AUC from 0.789 to 0.837, and a three-network 1-D convolutional model on nine raw channels caught about two thirds of pinches on unseen sessions, against 10--28\% for tree ensembles, firing about 0.4~s sooner. We report the system design, the data pipeline, the results and the evaluation pitfalls that inflated earlier results.
\end{abstract}
